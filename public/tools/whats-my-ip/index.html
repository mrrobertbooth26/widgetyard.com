Location is approximate. IP geolocation usually identifies a city or network region—not your device or street address. VPNs, mobile networks, and corporate gateways can show another location.

Why do I have both IPv4 and IPv6 at the same time?

Many modern connections use “dual stack,” which means they can reach the internet over both IPv4 and IPv6. Websites and apps choose an available route automatically. If the IPv6 check says unavailable, your device, router, VPN, or provider may currently be using IPv4 only.

What do ISP and ASN mean?

The provider or organization is the network that announces your public address. Its autonomous system number, or ASN, is the unique number used to identify that network when internet traffic is routed.

How accurate is the location?

IP location is an estimate based on network registration and routing data. It may identify a nearby city, your provider’s regional gateway, or the endpoint of a VPN. It should never be treated as a precise physical location.

Why might my IP address change?

Many providers assign dynamic addresses that can change after a router restart or over time. Switching Wi-Fi networks, using mobile data, or enabling a VPN can also change the public addresses shown here.
